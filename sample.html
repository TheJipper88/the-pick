Three things I'd say out loud

1. The bench stalled in the second session, not the first. 190×4 then 190×3 then 185×5 is a fatigue curve, not a strength ceiling. Two hard bench days in one week is the problem. The plan drops the second one to a light day and keeps the volume in a range you can actually finish.

2. Squat got one hard day and no backoff. 230×5 then 230×4 is a real top set, but the other squat day was three easy fives at 225. That's maintenance, not progress. Four weeks of five-pound steps needs both days doing something.

3. Row has the most room and gets it last. 155×8 puts the e1RM at 196 on a 181 lb frame, and it was the final movement of the week, already tired. That's the lift with the widest gap between what it's doing and what it could do.

What I don't do: chase a max off one week, change your technique from numbers alone, or give medical advice. If you've got a nagging joint or a coming meet, tell me and it changes the plan, not the price.

4. What one week can't tell me

Honest limits, because a plan built on a lie is worse than no plan. From one week I can see a fatigue pattern and a starting weight. I can't see whether the bench stall is volume, sleep, or a shoulder you haven't mentioned. Two weeks of log and the second block is sharper, because the trend line exists. Send three weeks and the read changes quality, not price.
